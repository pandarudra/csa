## Problem framing

**What "good" means for SpotifyCares support.** A message-level support
agent is trustworthy here if, for the 6 intents in `intents.yaml`: (1) it
classifies the customer's actual problem correctly often enough that
downstream routing/drafting isn't built on a wrong premise; (2) when it
drafts a reply, that reply is grounded in how SpotifyCares has actually
resolved similar issues before, not invented; and (3) it escalates to a
human whenever the situation needs account access, identity verification,
or a specific promise (refund, timeline) the agent cannot honestly make --
and does so *more* readily than it under-escalates, because a false
auto-handle is the costlier mistake (see Failure analysis).

**What was deliberately not built:**
- No live account actions (no real refunds, no password resets, no
  cancellations) -- the agent drafts and decides, it never executes.
- No multi-language support -- the sampled corpus is overwhelmingly
  English; building for languages barely represented in the training/
  grounding data would be evaluating something the system was never given
  evidence for.
- No handling of multi-customer "threads" (viral broadcast tweets with many
  unrelated repliers) -- these were filtered out of the corpus entirely
  during data prep (see DECISIONS.md #7) rather than modeled.
- No fine-grained sub-intents beyond the 6 in `intents.yaml` -- the
  taxonomy was deliberately kept small enough to classify and evaluate
  reliably (see `intents.yaml`'s header for how it was derived and merged).

## What is misleading about my headline number?

- **The golden set is small (150-250 hand-confirmed examples) and randomly
  sampled, not exhaustively representative** -- a headline accuracy/F1
  number is a point estimate with real sampling noise, especially per-intent
  (an intent with only ~15-20 test examples can swing several points from
  one or two mistakes).
- **The LLM judge and the reply generator are not fully independent** --
  both are the same NIM-hosted model family. A judge sharing failure modes
  or stylistic preferences with the generator it's scoring can systematically
  over-rate replies that are fluent but wrong in ways the judge itself
  wouldn't catch. `results/judge_agreement.json` (from `python -m
  src.eval_harness --human-judge` + `--judge-agreement`) is the check on
  this, not a guarantee it's fully absent.
- **The taxonomy is ours.** Intent accuracy is only as meaningful as the 6
  categories it's measured against; a message that doesn't fit any of them
  well gets forced into the closest one or the catch-all, and "accuracy"
  doesn't capture how often that forcing happens.
- **The dev/test split does double duty.** The same ~45-50 dev examples
  both tuned prompts/thresholds and trained the simple baseline's
  classifier -- the test split is untouched by tuning, but the simple
  baseline is deliberately data-starved in a way a production baseline
  wouldn't be, which flatters the full agent's relative advantage somewhat.
- **Historical replies used for grounding are themselves uneven quality**
  -- some are genuine troubleshooting, some are generic "please DM us"
  deflections (see DECISIONS.md #1 on why SpotifyCares was chosen partly to
  minimize this, but it isn't zero). A retrieved "similar case" being real
  doesn't mean it was actually a *good* resolution.
- **Latency/reliability on the free NIM endpoint is variable** -- some
  calls complete in ~1s, others take 30-45s and retry. A single live demo
  run's timing is not representative of a worst-case or production SLA.
